UNDERSTANDING THE POPULATION

Who lives with disability?

Before we ask whether services are accessible, we need to understand the people those services are meant to reach. Start with the national trend, then see how prevalence changes with age and which main health conditions are reported.

FIGURE 012009—2022 · AUSTRALIA

THE NATIONAL PICTURE

A stable decade, then a rise.

In 2022, an estimated 21.4% of Australians had disability—up from 17.7% in 2018. The graph shows five separate survey years, not an annual measurement.

WHAT TO NOTICE

After remaining between 17.7% and 18.5% over the four earlier surveys, prevalence reached 21.4% in 2022.

The increase may reflect multiple influences, including changes in reporting, awareness and survey methods—not necessarily a single cause.

HEALTHCARE ACCESS · 2022 SURVEY

Can people get the care they need?

Going to the doctor is one part of the picture. Missing out when care is needed is another. This chapter compares which health services people used, who experienced unmet need, what prevented access, and where daily assistance came from.

FIGURE 07 · PICTOGRAM GRIDAIHW / ABS SURVEY · 2022

USING HEALTHCARE

Most saw a GP—but other services were used less often.

Each panel contains 100 person symbols. Each coloured figure stands for 1% of people with disability who used the service in the previous year. Percentages appear beside service names for quick comparisons.

Loading pictograms…
93%

Almost all people with disability living in households saw a GP, but fewer saw a medical specialist (63%) or dental professional (51%). These activities can overlap; percentages are not parts of a whole.

Reading: one coloured silhouette = 1% of people with disability living in households. The chart shows visits for their own health during the preceding 12 months; it does not measure care quality. Source: AIHW (2026), Figure ACCESS.1 and supporting text (2022 SDAC).

WHEN PEOPLE MISS OUT

People with psychosocial disability report more missed care.

Each bar shows the percentage who needed a service but did not receive it at least once in the previous year. The dark tick marks the rate for all people with disability, so bars past the tick are above average. Click a group in the legend to highlight it.

Loading unmet-care chart…
+6 pp

For every service, people with psychosocial disability missed needed care 6 percentage points more often than people with disability overall (26% vs 20% for GPs). People with sensory or speech disability were 2–4 points below the overall rate.

The two disability groups can overlap with other disabilities and must not be added together. Values refer to people with disability living in households and having at least one episode of unmet need; these are not percentages of all medical appointments. Source: AIHW (2026), Figures ACCESS.2–ACCESS.3 and supporting text.

THE CHANGING NDIS · 2022–2026

Is disability support keeping pace?

Finally, look beyond individual snapshots. The number of NDIS participants has expanded—but how much allocated support funding people use varies by living-support needs. Growth in participation is not the same as successful access to every service.

Time period: each point uses the six months from 1 October of the preceding year to 31 March of the labelled year; the most recent April–June quarter is excluded because of payment-reporting lags. THE TAKEAWAY

More people are getting NDIS support.
Access and utilisation still differ.

Disability affects Australians of all ages, with markedly different experiences across groups and places. The NDIS is supporting more participants, but national expansion alone does not tell us whether people get all the healthcare and daily help they need. Understanding unmet needs and differing patterns of support is crucial.
